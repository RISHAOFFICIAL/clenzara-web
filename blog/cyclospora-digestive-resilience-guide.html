Cyclospora is a microscopic foodborne organism that has become an increasingly common summer wellness concern. Often linked to imported produce like berries, basil, cilantro, and lettuce, it can cause significant digestive disruption that lingers for days or even weeks. In this guide, we explore the Traditional Wellness perspective on Cyclospora — focusing on how to support your body's natural resilience and maintain biological harmony in the face of environmental challenges.

What is Cyclospora and How Does it Spread?

Cyclospora cayetanensis is a single-celled organism that is transmitted through the fecal-oral route, most commonly via contaminated food or water. Unlike many other foodborne concerns, Cyclospora is not typically spread directly from person to person. Instead, it requires time outside the host (usually in soil or water) to become infectious.

In the United States, outbreaks are most frequent during the spring and summer months, often coinciding with peak harvest and import seasons for fresh, hand-picked produce.

Common Sources of Exposure:

  • Fresh Berries: Raspberries and blackberries are historically linked to seasonal advisories.
  • Leafy Greens: Romaine, spinach, and pre-packaged salad mixes.
  • Fresh Herbs: Cilantro and basil (especially in restaurant settings).
  • Unfiltered Water: Well water or surface water in certain regions.

Prevention is the first step in any Traditional Wellness journey. Here is how to reduce your exposure and protect your system:

Maintain "Biological Order" in your kitchen. Use separate cutting boards for produce and proteins, and wash all utensils with hot, soapy water after contact with fresh produce.
